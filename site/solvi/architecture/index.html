Today

Six sources, joined by hand

  • The same customer is typed into three systems.
  • Eight risk factors on one battery job were never captured.
  • The monthly report takes days, and the numbers differ by who built it.
  • Nobody owns the data, so nobody fixes it.
  • Customer history is locked in the old ERP.
  • The board counts days without incident, which says nothing about tomorrow.
Target

Captured at the source, checked by its owner

New capture points close the gaps. Each domain checks its own data before it publishes, and one shared model feeds every app. A nightly pipeline runs the domains in order, core first and HSEQ last. The domain map shows how the domains share data with each other.

Roadmap

Three steps, each useful on its own

Step 1 cuts a thin slice through four domains to fix the most urgent problem first. Each step switches something old off, and ends with a decision on whether to go on.

Step 1 · months 1 to 3

Make battery jobs safe

Build
Core and HSEQ, plus the battery parts of supply and delivery: the goods receipt check and the job checklist. A risk score before every battery job, in the Safety and quality app.
Switch off
The risk log spreadsheet and the days without incident board.
Go on when
9 of 10 battery jobs have a score before work starts.
Step 2 · months 4 to 8

One version of the work

Build
The rest of supply and delivery, and assets, each with a named owner. Shortcuts instead of copies. The Service app: production against what the weather allowed.
Switch off
Supplier and crew spreadsheets, and production checks by hand.
Go on when
Owners fix their own gate stops within two working days.
Step 3 · months 9 to 14

One customer history

Build
Sales with the old ERP archive. Proposals, follow-up and one search across old and new, in the Sales app.
Switch off
Monthly reports built by hand.
Done when
The monthly report builds itself, and sales and finance agree on the numbers.

Pays back in year 3

Most of the value is safety. If incidents don't fall, the other savings pay for running the platform, but not for building it. That is why step 1 is the battery slice, and why it has its own decision point.

What has to be true

  • Serious battery incidents fall from about one a year to one every three years, at NOK 1.8 million each.
  • Crews use the checklist on every battery job.
  • Owners spend about two hours a week on their domain.

How

  • One workspace per domain. The owner is admin. Others read only the published gold tables, through shortcuts.
  • Row-level rules in the semantic model: sellers see their own area.
  • Pipelines run as a service principal, not as a person. Secrets live in Key Vault.
  • Customer names and addresses live only in core, so a deletion request is handled in one place.
  • Development and tests use generated data. Real data never leaves production.
