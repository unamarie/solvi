Today

Shared KPIs, local definitions

Solvi has the usual systems and a few KPIs everyone knows. But sellers, purchasers, planners and product owners each read the data their own way, and there is no agreed way to break the KPIs down to the work that moves them.

One KPI, three answers

On-time delivery last quarter, as each team reports it.

71 %SellersFinished in the week promised at signing. 29 of 100 jobs were late.
85 %PurchasersSupplier deliveries on the date the supplier confirmed. This measures suppliers, not the promise to the customer.
92 %Project planningStarted on the latest planned date. 21 of the 29 late jobs were re-planned before the promised week, so they count as on time.

Each number is right by its own rule. But a date that moves with the delay hides it, so the KPI looks green inside the company and red to the customer. Solvi's definition follows the customer: only a change the customer asks for moves the date.

  • Everyone knows the KPIs, but it's hard to say which jobs, suppliers or sellers move them.
  • The same KPI has several numbers, depending on who made the report.
  • Some cases are registered in the quality system, but not linked to the job, supplier or installation they concern.
  • Checklists from the field are free text, so they can't be counted.
  • Customer history is locked in the old ERP.
  • The safety KPI is days without incident, which shows the past, not the next job.
Target

Captured at the source, checked by its owner

New fields in the systems people already use close the gaps. Each domain checks its own data before it publishes, and one shared model holds every KPI and its drill-down. A nightly pipeline runs the domains in order, core first and HSEQ last. The domain map shows how the domains share data with each other.

Roadmap

Three steps, each useful on its own

Step 1 cuts a thin slice through four domains to fix the most urgent problem first. Each step retires something old, and ends with a decision on whether to go on.

Step 1 · months 1 to 3

Make battery jobs safe

Build
Core and HSEQ, plus the battery parts of supply and delivery: new fields in goods receipt and in the field app checklist. A risk score before every battery job, in the Safety and quality app.
Retire
Days without incident as the safety KPI.
Go on when
9 of 10 battery jobs have a score before work starts.
Step 2 · months 4 to 8

One version of the work

Build
The rest of supply and delivery, and assets, each with a named owner. One definition of on-time delivery and of a fault. The Service app: production against what the weather allowed.
Retire
Purchasers' own supplier lists, and each team's own version of on-time delivery.
Go on when
Owners fix their own gate stops within two working days.
Step 3 · months 9 to 14

One customer history

Build
Sales with the old ERP archive. Proposals, follow-up and one search across old and new, in the Sales app. Margin drills down from company to job.
Retire
Reconciling numbers before management meetings.
Done when
Sales, purchasing and finance show the same margin and on-time numbers, and each KPI opens down to the jobs behind it.

Most of the value is safety. If incidents don't fall, the other savings pay for running the platform, but not for building it. That is why step 1 is the battery slice, and why it has its own decision point.

What has to be true

  • Serious battery incidents fall from about one a year to one every three years, at NOK 1.8 million each.
  • Teams accept one definition per company KPI.
  • Crews use the checklist on every battery job.
  • Owners spend about two hours a week on their domain.

How

  • One workspace per domain. The owner is admin. Others read only the published gold tables, through shortcuts.
  • Row-level rules in the semantic model: sellers see their own area.
  • Pipelines run as a service principal, not as a person. Secrets live in Key Vault.
  • Customer names and addresses live only in core, so a deletion request is handled in one place.
  • Development and tests use generated data. Real data never leaves production.
