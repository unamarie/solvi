How the data is organised

Six domains, one shared core

Each domain has an owner, its own workspace and its own lakehouse. Shared master data lives once, in the core. Other domains read it through shortcuts instead of copying it, so a customer or a site is the same everywhere. Every domain runs its own quality gate before it publishes anything.

Why not one big model?

When everything sits in one place, nobody owns anything, and one change can break five reports. With domains, the people who know the data are responsible for it, and the rules for sharing are explicit.

Why shortcuts, not copies?

A copied customer table is out of date the day after. A shortcut always points to the owner's version, so every app sees the same customer, site and product.

Why a gate in every domain?

Errors are cheapest where they start. Each domain checks its own data, including links to other domains, before it publishes anything others depend on.

Why risk rules as data?

The risk factors, weights and stop rules are a table owned by quality. They can be tuned after the next near miss without changing any code.

Solvi is fictional. All customers, suppliers, jobs and events are generated by a script; places and weather patterns are real for realism. Built by Una Marie S. Goksøyr.
